Money Doors

07.2026

About the project

Money Doors was a product made in collaboration with a bank. The purpose of the game is to teach young teenagers to manage their finances better and avoid too many microtransactions like lootboxes, skins, or gambling sites. The game features multiple money-themed minigames which players complete within a time limit. The true lesson however comes from decisions made between the minigames by testing player's ability to plan their spending, manage risks, take good bargains, and save as much money as possible.

The gameplay loop is simple:
1. Add savings at the beginning of the game;. | 2. Chose a character (no impact for the game so far). | 3. Chose one of the three puzzles. | 4. Bargain with the host. | 5. Choose and pay for the difficulty. | 6. Play through the puzzle. | 7a. If completed: increase your savings. | 7b. If failed: lose money. | 8. Repeat from step 2 until rounds or money runs out. | 9. Show score.

This is a class diagram I've made to visualise how everything is tied together. The entirety of the diagram (except for individual puzzles) was implemented into the game by me. Puzzle Management

One of my proudest works is a Puzzle Manager. The manager will randomize and display all yet-unplayed puzzles in the door choice scene and show their names alongside their icons together with a debug tool that allows developers to play any puzzle. No code is required to add or remove puzzles from the list.

Making new puzzles was quick and easy for other developers. All they needed to do is make a new scene and script inheriting from original puzzle, determine what happens on puzzle_start(), and adjust it for every difficulty.

Player Data

I created a system that stored player's gameplay by tracking how well they did in each puzzle. Every time a player plays through a puzzle, the following data is stored: name of the puzzle, its difficulty, time to complete, and whether it was completed. When visiting statistics, the summary of all puzzles can be seen including each individual puzzle previously played.

This has not only allowed us to test the game more efficiently without needing to look at their gameplay all the time, but also gave the players' opportunity to replay the game and track their statistics.
